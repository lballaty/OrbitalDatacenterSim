Orbital AI Data Center Economics Model

First-principles scenario model for current AI racks in independently maneuverable satellites, larger modules, or persistent serviceable orbital platforms. Scope: inference workloads only; ground connectivity via leased relay on an existing communications constellation. Training is a separate problem (Tbit/s inter-satellite bandwidth, interruption tolerance, utilization) and is not modeled here.
Base architecture: one 135 kW / 72-GPU rack per satellite Cost regime: Traditional aerospace / low volume

Spare racks add mass and cost but do not generate productive tokens. The architecture selector changes the drawing only; economics are driven by racks per satellite and the replaceable-compute setting on the Costs tab.

Compute stack

—
Rack TDP, mass, accelerator count and $/rack live on the Architecture tab and are overwritten when a preset is selected; the stack multiplier overwrites the Spacecraft-tab radiation failure multiplier. Only the GB300 large-MoE throughput cell is a measured value (SemiAnalysis InferenceX, 116 tok/s/user); every other stack × model cell is an estimate and is labelled as such. Hot-spare nodes are unpowered and do not raise expected throughput; they convert stochastic degradation into a guaranteed capacity floor. Radiation multipliers are scenario values: no vendor has published LEO SEE data for current inference silicon; Google's Suncatcher proton tests covered Trillium, not Ironwood.

Served model and revenue

—
Prices are per-token API rates observed early September 2026 from first-party pages or neutral hosts; they move monthly. A third-party operator sells at market, so revenue is market price minus licence; the terrestrial figures on the Inference tab are cost, not price. Token traffic on the leased relay link is derived from delivered tokens, so small models load the link far harder than large MoE.

Orbit, access and congestion

Each cluster is one tight formation at a single point on its orbit (R_max of order 1–50 km), not satellites spread around the orbit. Planes and clusters are layout choices; clusters set how many formations the fleet is split into.
—
Drag Δv is now computed from configured density, drag coefficient, projected area and dry mass. Density is a mean-solar-activity value; solar maximum can raise it 3–10× at 400–650 km. Battery energy is sized on the worst-season eclipse, not the annual average. Projected drag fraction follows the orbit preset: sun-pointing arrays in dawn-dusk SSO fly close to edge-on to the velocity vector (~25%); sun-tracking arrays in inclined LEO average ~50%.
—
Cluster geometry follows Google Suncatcher (81 sats, R_min 100 m, R_max 1 km, planar rectangular lattice; satellites trace 2:1 relative ellipses in the Hill frame) and Penot & Balakrishnan (MIT, AAS 26-754, 2026): deployable count N ≈ a·(R_max/R_min)^b with a,b = 0.80,2 (Suncatcher), 3.63,2 (optimal planar hex, rigid rotation), 0.27,3 (3D). Self-shadowing begins when satellite footprint radius R_sat exceeds ≈0.5·R_min (rect), ≈0.19·R_min (hex), ≈0.03·R_min (3D). A nearest-neighbour ISL mesh does not scale; a VL2-like Clos fabric needs some satellites to act as switches carrying k ISLs and no compute — those are added to the fleet here.
Avoidance now depends on absolute shell occupancy rather than a ratio in which shell capacity cancels. The shell capacity remains a scenario threshold, not a claim that orbit has a fixed number of physical “slots.”

Power, thermal, control and propulsion

Core avionics, flight computer and transponder do not replicate per rack, but reaction-wheel/CMG authority, harness, tankage and structure grow with the larger vehicle. Bus mass = base + per-rack × (racks − 1); set per-rack to 0 to make control fully fixed.
130 Wh/kg is a pack-level figure for space-qualified Li-ion (cells + BMS + structure + thermal + radiation-tolerant electronics). 180 Wh/kg is cell-level; enter it only if the mass stack accounts for packaging elsewhere. Peak basis uses the rack peak input on the Architecture tab instead of TDP.
—
Reference: ISS EATCS rejects roughly 150 W/m² at about 14 kg/m² with a cold ammonia loop. 450 W/m² is defensible only for a hot coolant loop (~330–340 K) with two-sided radiators and a good view factor; 8 kg/m² is at the light end of deployable radiator studies (range roughly 5–12 kg/m²) and is chosen so the base satellite closes under the Falcon 9 SSO planning payload with thin margin. Set 10–12 kg/m² to see the base case fail to close.
Characteristic span is the square-equivalent size of the combined solar + radiator area (both wings). It is checked against a stowed/deployment envelope, against formation spacing (a span wider than the spacing breaks the collision-avoidance envelope), and area-to-mass is checked against an agility limit above which large flexible structures dominate attitude control and pointing. These are screening flags, not a flexible-body dynamics model.
A satellite retired under control disposes far more reliably than one that has already failed; a dead bus may have lost the propulsion or attitude control needed to deorbit. These are now separate.
A multi-tonne satellite carrying steel radiator plumbing, a large battery and a GPU rack will not demise on uncontrolled re-entry; casualty-risk limits (~1:10,000) then require a targeted re-entry, which needs a high-thrust final burn after the electric spiral. Disposal occurs at every scheduled retirement and at every failure; failed disposals leave derelicts that are either removed at the ADR price or accumulate in the shell over the analysis horizon. The 5-year post-mission disposal clock starts at failure, not at design end of life.
Google has published proton testing for Trillium TPU/HBM against a five-year SSO dose target; that does not qualify GB300/HBM. Radiation multipliers here are explicit scenario assumptions so that the risk cannot disappear into shielding mass alone.

Manufacturing and launch

Structure and panels tend to learn faster (lower LR) than flight electronics. In split mode each manufacturing group walks its own curve against the same cumulative unit count; single mode applies the default rate to all.
A GB300-class, ~13 t spacecraft with large deployable arrays, active two-phase thermal control and a targeted-re-entry stage is plausibly $0.5–1B to develop and qualify. Default raised to $500M; sweep it in the sensitivity chart.
Wright learning curve: unit N costs C_ref·(N/N_ref)^log₂(LR). It applies to the five spacecraft manufacturing inputs (not compute, which is already a volume market) and runs continuously through initial deployment, scheduled replacements and failure replacements, so a 1 GW build prices unit 9,000 far below unit 9. NRE is a one-off at t=0, not insured, and is reported per satellite over the horizon. Each cost preset states the cumulative unit its prices refer to, so preset and curve do not double count.
Traditional aerospace values are intentionally expensive and represent low-volume spacecraft-style procurement. The mass-production preset is illustrative, not a published Starlink unit-cost claim.
The 13.5 t SSO value is a planning value informed by demonstrated reusable polar Starlink payloads around 14.4 t to lower insertion orbits; it is not an official direct-to-650 km performance guarantee.
The optical relay terminal is separate spacecraft hardware (mass + cost), not folded into the compute rack. The lease can be a flat per-satellite fee or derived from provisioned Gbit/s at a monthly rate — third-party optical-ISL access is priced per capacity, so the per-Gbit/s basis is usually the honest one.
Scope: ground connectivity is assumed to be leased capacity on an existing communications constellation (Starlink-class or equivalent) via an optical inter-satellite link. No ground-station build is modeled. The relay terminal itself sits in network/storage mass per rack; third-party optical-terminal access is proprietary and priced, so this line is a placeholder lease fee. Inference only; training is out of scope for this model.

Inference, data movement and latency

InferenceX GB300 curves show roughly 2× throughput loss going from ~70 to ~150 tok/s/user, which is α ≈ 0.9 on a power law. The factor applies to both the orbital and terrestrial stacks (same silicon) and is clamped to 0.2–3. At the reference interactivity the factor is 1, so the regression anchor is unaffected.
The terrestrial stack reuses the same racks, rack cost, IT overhead, spares, utilization, throughput, refresh interval, horizon and discount rate as the orbital case, so the two $/1M-token figures are annuitized identically. Facility $/MW covers shell, power distribution and cooling. Land, grid-connection queue cost and carbon are excluded.

Model integrity notes

v5.5: compute stack and served model become separate presets (Compute stack and Served model tabs) joined by a stack × model throughput matrix. Only GB300 × large MoE is a measured cell; every other cell is an estimate and the badge on the throughput input says so. SRAM stacks with trillion-parameter models are "not servable" and drive throughput to zero rather than interpolating. Node-level sparing: unpowered hot spares set a guaranteed capacity floor; expected node failures over life reduce delivered tokens. Revenue view for a third-party operator: blended market price minus licence minus orbital delivered cost. Relay-link traffic is now derived from delivered tokens; the former traffic input is "other traffic". Regression anchor unchanged (test forces node failure 0). Also: interactivity scaling of the throughput cell (power law, α = 0.9 from InferenceX GB300 curves, applied to both stacks); first-party vs neutral-host price source per model; radiation-multiplier sensitivity strip (×1.25 / ×2.5 / ×5) in the Stack × model panel.

Manufacturing regime: the result is highly sensitive to spacecraft manufacturing cost. The preset is displayed prominently and all $/kg inputs remain editable.

Thermal correction (v2): radiator areal density default raised from 5 to 10 kg/m² and a heat-transport mass line (kg/kW) added; both feed the thermal subsystem cost. The rack mass remains a terrestrial chassis figure and is not adjusted for space packaging.

Drag and eclipse correction (v2): stationkeeping Δv is derived from density × velocity² × Cd × projected area / dry mass rather than a preset constant, and battery energy is sized on the worst-season eclipse per orbit. Drag is computed against dry mass, which slightly understates propellant.

Salvage correction (v2): replacements that fall inside the analysis horizon but outlive it receive a straight-line residual credit at the horizon, so a year-9 replacement in a 10-year horizon no longer carries full cost.

Low-sun orbit correction: solar sizing now powers the load during sunlight and replaces eclipse energy through a configurable charge efficiency. Battery capacity is divided by usable depth of discharge.

Congestion correction: avoidance demand now rises with absolute occupancy pressure against the configured shell capacity.

Spare correction: spare racks are installed capacity only; they do not generate productive tokens in the throughput calculation.

Base-case closure (v3): radiator areal density default lowered to 8 kg/m² and the 650 km worst-season eclipse to 15 min so the default satellite closes under the 13.5 t Falcon 9 SSO planning payload. Margin is thin by design; raising either value shows the mass problem immediately.

Module refresh launch (v3): compute-module refresh uses its own modules-per-launch volume limit instead of the whole-satellite limit, which previously priced module refresh at roughly 3× the whole-satellite launch cost per kg.

Failure delay (v3): expected capacity unavailable while failed satellites await replacement now reduces effective availability instead of being display-only.

Drag geometry (v3): projected drag fraction is set per orbit preset (dawn-dusk SSO ~25%, inclined LEO ~50%).

v5.4: single true-scale view by default: Earth radius sets the scale, wheel zoom runs from LEO-shell detail (Earth ~1,000 px) out to the Moon at its true 60.3 Earth radii and true 0.27 R size, so the separate Moon mode is gone (LEO ×4 and log-compressed remain as options). Sun drawn as a glowing disc at the canvas edge in the true Sun direction with barely-transparent sunlight streams to Earth and to the Moon (distance to the Sun not to scale). Moon position is a low-precision phase estimate, not an ephemeris. Guide rings adapt to the zoom level.

v5.3: realism pass. Epoch defaults to today and the UTC scrubber to the current time, with a ◉ Now button. Propagation adds J2 secular rates (Ω̇, ω̇, Ṁ) so node drift is right over a day. The modeled constellation is now generated as GP-style element sets (RAAN from LTAN and Sun for SSO, satellites dithered within the cluster arc) and propagated by the same code as catalog objects — they are the only synthetic objects drawn once a catalog is present, labelled "(modeled, not real)", and can be exported as GP JSON (NORAD IDs 900000+, COMMENT marks them synthetic). On a served origin the same-origin ./gp.json loads silently at startup; CelesTrak-direct remains manual.

v5.2: fix: in pop-out windows the CSS that hides the view-selector buttons also matched the 3D pane's scale, time-scrubber and live-catalog control bars, so a popped-out 3D view had no controls. Rule now scoped to the main view bar only.

v4.5: optional live catalog: a button fetches CelesTrak's active-object GP JSON (or a downloaded copy can be dropped in via the file loader when CORS/offline blocks the fetch), compacts it to eight fields per object, caches it in localStorage for 24 h, bins objects by mean altitude into the six shells (replacing the embedded snapshot counts and re-dating the shells view), and draws them in the 3D view with two-body Keplerian propagation (Newton–Raphson Kepler solve) driven by the epoch date and UTC scrubber; coloured by operator, click-to-identify. Two-body propagation ignores J2 node drift (~5°/day for Starlink) — adequate for a same-day view.

v4.4: 3D modeled-orbit plane now placed from the Sun: for sun-synchronous orbits the ascending node is at Sun RA + (LTAN−12)·15°, so the base LTAN 06:00 orbit lies on the terminator as it should (previously the node was fixed on the x-axis, making the plane's orientation relative to day/night arbitrary). Non-SSO planes drift at the J2 nodal rate. Eclipse calculations were already using the correct node; only the drawing was wrong.

v4.3: cluster model grounded in Google Suncatcher (81 sats, R_min 100 m, R_max 1 km, planar rectangular lattice, 2:1 relative ellipses) and Penot & Balakrishnan (MIT 2026: optimal planar hex 3.63·(R_max/R_min)², 3D 0.27·(R_max/R_min)³, self-shadow limits 0.5/0.19/0.03·R_min, Clos fabric with dedicated switch satellites). Inputs: cluster design, R_min, clusters, Clos on/off, k, switch mass/cost fractions. Derived: R_max, footprint R_sat vs shadow limit, Clos layers, switch satellite count. Switch satellites are added to the fleet (no racks; fractional mass and non-compute cost; k/2 relay terminals) and enter launch, ops, disposal and congestion. Cluster view redrawn in the Hill frame to scale with footprints, relative-orbit ellipse and compute→switch links; 3D view draws each cluster as one point (a 1.7 km formation is 0.02% of the orbit radius). Findings: an ~80 m tip-to-tip satellite (R_sat 40 m) fits the rectangular lattice only at R_min ≥ ~100 m and self-shadows in the hex and 3D designs; 1 GW in one Suncatcher-type cluster at R_min 500 m needs R_max ≈ 53 km.

Disposal (v3.1): a disposal method selector adds a chemical targeted-re-entry stage (hardware mass plus an impulsive burn from ~200 km to the target perigee, computed by vis-viva) when dry mass exceeds a demisability threshold. Disposal operations are charged per event.
